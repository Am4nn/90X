short on purpose.

Settled, with the reasoning, in DECISIONS.md: the 11 primitives and 47 archetypes;

## The four things left, all measurements rather than decisions

1. **Does a structured card cost more to generate than a prose one?** The original pass
   averaged about $0.0017 per card, and structured formats carry more fields.
   **Run one topic, then set the budget.**

2. **Is the blind gate's rejection rate plausible?** Three samples should reject the genuinely
   guessable and few others. **Run 50 cards.** If it rejects half, the gate is wrong, not the
   corpus.

3. **Are the wrong reasons in a why-step actually plausible?** If they are not, marking a
   correct answer wrong because of the reason punishes a writing failure rather than a
   knowledge gap. **This is the first thing to judge in the ~94-card review**, not something to
   discover from a complaint.

4. **Item counts per primitive** — pairs in a match, rows in a claim grid, steps in an order.
   A layout question at 390px as much as a pedagogy one, and it interacts with no partial
   credit: eight pairs marked all-or-nothing is punishing. **Decide from the mocks.**

None of these blocks writing the implementation plan.
